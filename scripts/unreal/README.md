# Unreal scripts (phase 1)

Settings: the folders and names come from the workspace's `pipeline_config.json` (made by `create_workspace.py`): `unreal.wip_folder`, `unreal.completed_folder`, `unreal.character_folder`, `unreal.metahuman_blueprint`, `paths.unreal_exports`, `paths.metahuman_export`, `paths.video_source` and `video.action`. Each script's settings at its top are left as `None` and take those; a value written there wins. Without a config the scripts use Unreal's own folders (`/Game/WIP`, `/Game/Completed`, `/Game/MetaHumans`, `<project>/Saved/...`). Keep `pipeline_config.py` in the same folder as the scripts (`create_workspace.py` puts them all in the workspace's `Scripts`).

- `check_plugins.py` - checks that the plugins in `unreal.required_plugins` (pipeline_config.json) are installed and enabled; Output Log + message box. Other scripts can call `check_plugins.check_required_plugins()`.
- `list_plugins.py` - lists the plugins enabled in the open project; also saved to `Saved/plugin_list.txt`.
- `unreal_procedural_fixes.py` - Unreal version of the Blender procedural fixes, for a MetaHuman baked to `MetaHuman_ControlRig` in Sequencer: matches the IK controls to the baked animation, switches arms and legs to IK, plants the feet, and adds a "Neutral pose" layer that blends the clip from and back to the neutral pose. One undo step, safe to run again; report saved to `Saved/procedural_fixes_report.txt`. Settings are at the top of the file.
- `capture_neutral_pose.py` - saves the pose on the current frame as `neutral_pose.json`, the neutral pose the fixes ease from and to. Capture once, use for every clip.
- `probe_face_data.py` - reports where the facial data is in the project: the curves on each MetaHuman Animation Sequence, the MetaHuman Performance assets, and what this engine version offers for exporting animation from them; saved to `Saved/face_probe.txt`.
- `run_probe.py` - runs `probe_unreal_layers.py` from the same folder and copies its report next to it.
- `probe_unreal_layers.py` - reports what Unreal's Python can do with Control Rig animation layers in this engine version, and lists the rig's controls; saved to `Saved/unreal_probe.txt`.
- `export_animations.py` - batch export for Blender. It looks only in the project's `Content/WIP` folder (`SOURCE_PATH`; Unreal shows it as `/Game/WIP`, and the setting takes either the disk path or the Content Browser path); each folder directly under it is a take. For every Animation Sequence there it writes the skeleton and animation as FBX (`<name>_body.fbx`), the animation's curves as JSON (`<name>_body_curves.json`; MetaHuman Animator's face expression curves, `CTRL_expressions_*`, are in here) and an `export_manifest.json`, then runs `export_face_board.py` for the same takes. Each take's source video is then moved from the video folder (`paths.video_source`) into its export folder, next to its FBX files, so the video travels with the take into Blender: it is found through the take's own assets and by name (the date, take number and `SGM`/`ASL` left out, so `ASL_Jesus_tk1` finds `2026-10-15 SGM ASL Jesus.mp4`; the newest when a sign was recorded twice), or set in `VIDEO_MAP`. `VIDEO_ACTION = "copy"` leaves the original where it is; `VIDEOS_ONLY = True` only adds the videos to take folders already exported. Every take that was exported completely (all its animations, and the face of its Performance) is then moved to `Content/Completed` (`COMPLETED_PATH`, `/Game/Completed`) and saved there; the others stay, with the reason in the log. Both folders are created when missing. Exports to `paths.unreal_exports` (one folder per take).
- `export_face_board.py` - the face for Blender's face board. For every MetaHuman Performance it exports a Level Sequence onto the MetaHuman with the face keyed on the face control board (`LS_FaceBoard_<performance>`, created next to the Performance and rebuilt on each run) and writes that track as a Control Rig FBX (`<performance>_face.fbx`) and as numbers (`<performance>_face_board.json`). Run by `export_animations.py`, or on its own for every Performance under `/Game/WIP` (it never moves takes). Has to sit in the same folder as `export_animations.py`.
- `import_blender_takes.py` - the way back: takes finished in Blender (Export FBX in the Metahuman Rig tab writes `<name>.fbx`, `<name> Face Board.json` and `<name> Export.json`) into the project. A window lists them, with a search box; tick **Import body** (an Animation Sequence on the MetaHuman's body skeleton), **Import face** (the face board keyed onto the MetaHuman's face control board; without a MetaHuman, the face skeleton's FBX) and **Add to MetaHuman** (picked from the MetaHumans in the open level: a Level Sequence `LS_<take>` with it playing the take). Everything goes to `/Game/Finished/<take>/` (`unreal.finished_folder`).
- `export_metahuman.py` - the MetaHuman itself for Blender: the body and face skeletal meshes of the project's MetaHuman (`unreal.metahuman_blueprint`, else the one in `unreal.character_folder`; read from its Body and Face components) as FBX, to `paths.metahuman_export/<blueprint>/`, with `metahuman_export.json`. Run once; `build_metahuman_rig.py` in Blender builds the character from it. It also writes the textures of the body, face and outfit materials (`textures/`, PNG; the face's wrinkle and region maps are left out, `TEXTURE_SKIP`) and the hair cards of the blueprint's grooms - Hair, Eyebrows, Beard and so on, the lowest LOD with cards, as FBX with their textures (`hair/`; `HAIR_SKIP` leaves out the peach fuzz). Strand-only grooms can't be exported and are named in the log. `metahuman_export.json` lists every material slot with its textures, and every hair card mesh.
- `Ingest.py` - the labelled videos (`paths.video_source`) into Unreal: each new video is ingested with Capture Manager as a mono video take, moved into its own take folder `/Game/WIP/<take>` (`unreal.wip_folder`), and given a MetaHuman Performance (`MHP_<take>`) with body tracking (`unreal.body_tracking`) set. No Identity is needed - each mono video take is solved from the video alone, as clips of different people need; `unreal.identities` (`{text in the take's name: Identity}`) or `unreal.identity` (every take) gives one to a performer who has it. The take is named after the video, with dates and `video.slate_drop_words` left out: `SGM Hello 2026-10-15.mp4` -> `Hello_tk1`; a second recording of the same sign becomes `_tk2`. Which take each video became is kept in `Logs/ingested_videos.json`, so a run only ingests new videos (`ON_EXISTING = "redo"` ingests them again), and `export_animations.py` uses it to give each take exactly its own video. Capture Manager's converted footage goes to `paths.capture_media`.
- `Process.py` - solves every unsolved Performance under `/Game/WIP` with MetaHuman Animator, one after the other (it solves one at a time; several minutes each), saves it and exports its Animation Sequence (`AS_<take>`, performer skeleton, face and body) into the take folder. The editor stays usable; progress is in the Output Log and a box says when the batch is done. `REPROCESS = True` solves solved ones again.
- `move_metahuman_character.py` - moves MetaHuman Character assets (MetaHuman Creator's; `export_metahuman.py` reads the head DNA from them) out of take folders into `unreal.character_folder` (`/Game/MetaHumans`), fixing up references. `export_animations.py` now does the same for any it finds in a take before moving the take to Completed (`CHARACTER_FOLDER`).
